## ⚠️ PRIORITY 1 — Headline finding

### **Bilateral scrotal fluid collections + possible bilateral hydroceles / spermatoceles**

**Where:** Series 9 (Ax T1 FSE pelvis) panel mid-stack and Series 14 (WATER COR T2 FSE Flex) panel #35.

**What's visible:** Both scrotal compartments contain rounded fluid-signal collections adjacent to / surrounding the testicles. On the fat-suppressed T2 coronal they are very bright (consistent with simple fluid). On T1 they are dark (consistent with water, not blood or proteinaceous fluid).

**Possible interpretations:**
- **Bilateral hydroceles** (most common cause — fluid in the tunica vaginalis around each testicle)
- **Spermatoceles** (cysts of the epididymis containing sperm)
- **Epididymal cysts**
- **Varicoceles** (less likely — those would be serpiginous vessels, not round collections)
- **Reactive effusion** from any inflammatory/infectious process in the scrotum

**Why check with a doctor:** Any scrotal mass in a man your age (26) needs **clinical correlation** — the radiologist cannot tell from MRI alone whether a fluid collection is benign or something requiring urology referral. The fact that it's **bilateral** is unusual; unilateral hydroceles are very common but bilateral raise a small concern for:
- Inflammatory process (epididymo-orchitis, often from STI in this age group — chlamydia, gonorrhea)
- Trauma history
- Cardiac/ hepatic cause (less likely at 26)
- Tumor (much less likely if pure fluid on all sequences)

**What to ask:** "Can you please order a scrotal ultrasound with Doppler? The MRI showed bilateral fluid collections around the testicles — I need a urology-grade ultrasound to characterize them and rule out any solid component." *Scrotal ultrasound is the standard of care for testicular/scrotal pathology — MRI is not.*

**Priority: HIGH.**

## PRIORITY 2 — Lumbar spine findings

### Modic-type endplate changes / disc degeneration
**Where:** Series 3 (Sag T2 frFSE) and Series 4 (Sag T1 FSE) — visible on multiple lumbar levels, particularly lower lumbar.

**What's visible:**
- Several lumbar discs appear darker than expected on T2 (loss of normal bright nucleus signal = disc desiccation / degeneration). Most pronounced at L4-L5 and L5-S1.
- Some endplates show signal change consistent with **Modic changes** (Type 2 = fatty, bright on T1 and isointense/dark on T2; possibly Type 1 = edema, bright on T2 and dark on T1).
- Disc height appears mildly reduced at the lower lumbar levels.

**Clinical meaning:** Disc desiccation at age 26 is **early but not rare**. Often asymptomatic. Modic changes can be associated with low back pain but the correlation is imperfect. Can be related to:
- Genetic predisposition
- Mechanical stress (heavy labor, prolonged sitting, vibration exposure)
- Smoking
- Prior injury / microtrauma

**Priority: MEDIUM** — discuss with your doctor, especially if you have back pain.

## What this MRI was *designed* to evaluate vs what it actually showed

The protocol name says **"RMN COL LUMBAR + PELVIS OSEA"** — lumbar + bony pelvis. The Dixon fat/water technique was included, which is unusual for a routine lumbar protocol and usually signals that the ordering clinician was specifically interested in **bone marrow composition** (e.g. ruling out infiltrative marrow disease, assessing marrow fat fraction, looking for fatty marrow conversion). This is most often done in:
- Workup for unexplained back pain with constitutional symptoms
- Suspicion for **axial spondyloarthritis / sacroiliitis**
- Suspicion for **multiple myeloma** or marrow infiltrative disease
- Hematology/oncology staging

**If none of these apply to you, ask your doctor why the Dixon was ordered** — there may be clinical context you're missing.
