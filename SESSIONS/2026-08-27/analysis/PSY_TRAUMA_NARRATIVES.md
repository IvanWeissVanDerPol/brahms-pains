## Gaby's trauma — **Highest priority**

### What surfaced
- **Father died suddenly in her presence while she was driving** (+6813s): "Mi papá falleció conmigo a mi lado. O sea, yo estaba manejando y yo iba así, ¡pum! [palmada] se cayó y falleció acá en mi relación."
- **RCP-trained** ("profesional de blanco, yo sé hacer RCP") — chose a profession that lets her save people
- **Self-blame** for years: "yo mucho tiempo me culpé porque yo, yo sé hacer RCP. Y no, o sea, evidente no fue mi culpa, pero mucho tiempo era quilombo" (+6826s)
- "Mis pobres cerebros" — **plural brain-states**, suggesting she experiences herself as multiple (potential dissociative feature)
- "Cornearon asquerosamente" — graphic bodily language about how the trauma was inflicted (on her psyche)

### Stage: **Mourning (unfinished)**
Herman's stage 2: confronting the loss. Gaby has begun mourning her father but **the self-blame component is unprocessed**. She says "evidente no fue mi culpa" but then "mucho tiempo era quilombo" — the **rational acceptance is there, the felt acceptance isn't**.

### Connection to the pattern
**The caregiving compulsion is directly traceable to this trauma:**
- Before: father died in her arms while she was *driving* — **she couldn't stop**
- Now: she shows up at the hospital, she takes the 80 calls, she runs the household — **she can't stop because the next person might die**
- "Demasiado dulce" is the **post-trauma persona** — if she is sweet enough, attentive enough, the next death won't happen

### The autism disclosure (+6871s)
"Autis..." cut off. This is **a second major trauma** in the same session — late-diagnosed neurodivergence is itself a **re-narrative event** that recasts her entire life. The room dropped it. **It deserves its own session.**

### Recommended work
1. **EMDR or somatic experiencing** for the father's death (Herman stage 2)
2. **Late-autism processing** — find a specialist in adult-diagnosed autism (not pediatric framing)
3. **Trauma-informed therapy** with a clinician who can hold both the death and the autism re-narrative
4. **Body work** — she describes the trauma graphically in body language ("cornearon", "pum", "palmada"); the body is still processing
